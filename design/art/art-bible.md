## 1. Visual Identity Statement

### One-Line Visual Rule

> **"Every visual choice must make the character simultaneously inviting and illegible — beautiful enough to draw the player in, ambiguous enough that they cannot be sure they understand what they're looking at."**

When a pose, expression, or color could read as either open or guarded — choose guarded. When a composition could be flattering or slightly unsettling — choose both.

### Style Direction: Webtoon-Influenced Hybrid with Otome DNA

*Not pure otome CG, not pure webtoon — a disciplined synthesis:*

- **Structure from webtoon**: Strong silhouettes, high-contrast linework, thumbnail-legible compositions, character-specific graphic color palettes
- **Detail from otome**: Proportion conventions (slightly elongated, stylized), expressive face work, hair/clothing as character expression, emotional rendering fidelity in CG scenes

**Core structural innovation — Palette as Revelation**: Each character has a *public palette* (controlled, polished, slightly cool) and a *private palette* (warmer, more saturated, specific to their interiority). Art transitions toward the private palette as secrets emerge. This makes Principle 2 systematically implementable across all 20–30 characters.

### Supporting Principles

**Principle 1: The Public Face Is a Performance**
Every character's default sprite and CG presentation is a curated, slightly too-perfect version of themselves — styled, composed, and lit as though they know they're being seen.

*Design test*: When a character's idle expression is ambiguous between "relaxed" and "holding something back" — choose holding something back. A faint tension in the jaw, eyes that don't fully soften, hands positioned rather than resting.

*Pillar served*: Pillar 1 — Every Face Hides a Face.

---

**Principle 2: Revelation Has a Visual Register**
Private-self moments must look visually distinct from public-self moments — not just emotionally different but stylistically different, as though the art direction itself relaxes when the mask comes off.

*Design test*: When designing a vulnerability scene and unsure how far to push visual contrast — push further. Loosen the linework relative to polished public sprites, shift the palette toward the character's private color, let imperfection (disheveled collar, asymmetric framing) signal that something real is happening.

*Pillar served*: Pillar 2 — You Choose How Deep.

---

**Principle 3: Shareability Is a Compositional Requirement**
Every CG and key character moment must be designed to work as a standalone image — legible at thumbnail scale, with a clear focal point, a single dominant emotion, and no element that requires surrounding context to be impactful.

*Design test*: When composing a CG and unsure whether to include environmental context or tighten on the character — test the portrait crop first. If the portrait-cropped version reads as a complete, emotionally resonant image, the composition works. If the tight crop loses the image, there are too many competing centers of attention.

*Pillar served*: Pillar 4 — Designed to Be Shared.

## 2. Mood & Atmosphere

### State Map

Each game state occupies a distinct position on two axes: **intimacy** (distance between player and character) and **legibility** (how clearly the character's inner state can be read). Visual atmosphere places the player precisely on those axes and makes the movement between states felt.

| State | Temperature | Contrast | Palette Mode | Energy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| First Meeting | Cool-neutral | Low | Public (100%) | Measured |
| Surface Relationship | Warm | Low-mid | Public (polished) | Measured |
| The Dig | Mixed | Mid-high | Public + private interference | Charged |
| Revelation | Warm/private | High | Private (100%) | Still |
| Private Moment | Very warm | Low | Private (worn) | Contemplative |
| Main Menu / Creator | Neutral + accents | Medium | Public (theatrical) | Measured |
| Bad End | Cold | Low (flat) | Public (desaturated) | Foreclosed |

---

### State 1: First Meeting

**Mood target**: Curated intrigue — the feeling of encountering someone who is performing confidence so precisely you cannot tell if the performance is intentional.

**Lighting**: Midday or bright interior. Cool-neutral temperature (4800–5500K). High ambient fill, low shadow depth. The character is fully lit with almost no occlusion.

**Descriptors**: Polished, exposed, composed, legible-on-the-surface, slightly airless.

**Energy**: Measured. Everything the character does looks considered.

**Mood carrier**: Overexposure as concealment. The scene is lit too evenly — no dramatic shadows, no moody backlight. The character is presented plainly. Unease comes not from darkness but from the sense that even under full light you are reading a surface, not a person.

---

### State 2: Surface Relationship (Depth Tiers 1–2)

**Mood target**: Comfortable distance — warmth that feels genuine but never tips into unguarded. The pleasure of good company you do not fully trust.

**Lighting**: Golden hour, soft afternoon, or warm interior. Slightly warm temperature (5000–5800K). Low-to-mid contrast. Soft shadows, no hard edges.

**Descriptors**: Warm, practiced, pleasant, slightly staged, reassuring.

**Energy**: Measured to contemplative. Time feels comfortable and unhurried.

**Mood carrier**: The light source is consistent and flattering. Every surface relationship scene uses a portrait-quality light the character seems accustomed to — as though they have learned where to stand so the light falls well. Beauty that poses is still posing.

---

### State 3: The Dig

**Mood target**: Charged discomfort — the tension of a conversation that has turned a corner neither party can pretend hasn't happened. Attraction and wariness in the same breath.

**Lighting**: Transitional time of day (late afternoon into blue hour, or a lamp turned on as daylight fades). Mixed temperature: cooler ambient + warmer directional source creating competing illumination. Mid-to-high contrast. Shadows deepen but remain readable.

**Descriptors**: Charged, off-balance, alert, liminal, close.

**Energy**: Charged. The scene is quiet but the stillness is effortful.

**Mood carrier**: The character's public-palette colors begin to show faint interference from their private palette. Not a full shift — a tonal contamination. A secondary private-palette color appears in the lighting or a reflected surface. The character's face enters the mixed-light zone: half cool, half warm. The composition visually enacts the ambiguity of the moment.

---

### State 4: Revelation

**Mood target**: Exposed vulnerability — something real surfacing against the character's will or design. Not catharsis yet. The moment just before breath is released.

**Lighting**: Single dominant source, directional and close. Warm temperature (3000–3800K). High contrast, strong shadow falloff. The dominant source corresponds to the character's private palette anchor color. The rest of the scene drops toward near-black or deep desaturated background.

**Descriptors**: Stark, close, undefended, irreversible, still.

**Energy**: Contemplative descending into still. Time has slowed.

**Mood carrier**: The private palette completes its takeover of the lighting. For the first time in the playthrough, the character is lit entirely in their private color. The linework on the revealing sprite is loosened relative to all prior appearances — less precision, softer face rendering. The too-perfect quality is gone.

---

### State 5: Private Moment

**Mood target**: Quiet recognition — the rare pleasure of seeing someone be themselves when they have forgotten to be anything else. Tender without sentiment.

**Lighting**: Intimate ambient — late night interior, soft dawn window light, or candlelight equivalent. Very warm temperature (2700–3200K). Low contrast with gentle gradients. No harsh light anywhere.

**Descriptors**: Unguarded, soft, incidental, intimate, unhurried.

**Energy**: Contemplative. Unhurried to the point of stillness.

**Mood carrier**: Private palette at full saturation — but worn, not performed. The linework is at its loosest in the entire game (closest to sketch quality). Small imperfections are present and unremarked: a collar undone, hair unprepared, an expression not legible as a single emotion. *This is the only state where Principle 3 (Shareability) is intentionally relaxed — the scene feels caught, not staged, and resists being designed for an audience. The exception earns its place: the one image that does not perform for anyone is the most intimate reward.*

---

### State 6: Character Creator / Main Menu

**Mood target**: Anticipatory curiosity — the pleasant tension of a story not yet begun. Standing outside a party and hearing the music.

**Lighting**: Theatrical. Even controlled light with deliberate spotlight framing. Neutral-to-cool temperature with high-saturation accent points. Medium contrast.

**Descriptors**: Theatrical, curated, inviting, unknowable, gallery-still.

**Energy**: Measured. Unhurried presentation — the secrets will wait.

**Mood carrier**: Every character in full public palette at maximum intentionality. The meta space is the one place characters know they are being seen and have prepared completely. Cool dominant space makes any private-palette slip feel significant.

---

### State 7: Bad End / Closed Off

**Mood target**: Foreclosed regret — a door closed from the inside. Not grief. The recognition that something possible is no longer possible.

**Lighting**: Flat, cool, receding. Color temperature drops to cold (6500K+). Low contrast — the flatness reads as withdrawal, not softness. Colors desaturated toward grey.

**Descriptors**: Withdrawn, flat, distancing, final, grey.

**Energy**: Contemplative but foreclosed. Still in a way that is not peaceful.

**Mood carrier**: Character returns to public palette — but desaturated. Private palette glimpsed earlier is gone. The composed quality that read as intriguing in State 1 now reads as deliberate exclusion. The distinction between "performing for you" and "refusing to perform for you" is carried entirely by expression and color temperature.

## 3. Shape Language

### 3.1 Character Silhouette Philosophy

**Core rule: The silhouette tells you who they want to be. The details tell you who they are.**

**Thumbnail legibility standard**: All sprites must be silhouette-readable at 80×160px. At that scale, one shape quality must be unmistakable per character — the *silhouette signature*.

| Signature | Communicates | Use for |
|---|---|---|
| Vertical dominance (tall, slim, minimal horizontal break) | Controlled, self-contained, hard to approach | Characters projecting authority or restraint |
| Horizontal weight in upper body (broad shoulders, statement coat) | Social presence, occupies space deliberately | Characters whose mask is about being seen |
| Asymmetry (bag strap, hand out of pocket, hair sweep) | Readable energy, approachability — but instability | Characters who perform warmth as their mask |
| Compact silhouette with high-detail center | Forces the eye inward — intimacy through compression | Characters whose mask is quietness |
| Irregular perimeter (hair volume, layered clothing, wide sleeves) | Uncertain space — difficult to categorize | Characters who resist categorization |

No two characters share the same primary silhouette signature. Assigned in the character sheet before design begins. Inviolable.

**Base-model prevention rules**:
1. **Proportion tier diversity**: Three tiers — Narrow (<0.6× head-width shoulder ratio), Standard (0.6–0.8×), Wide (>0.8×). Each tier must contain ≥30% of cast.
2. **Distinctive feature rule**: Every character has one structurally unusual feature (jawline, eye set, nose specificity) visible at thumbnail scale. Built into face geometry, not accessories.
3. **Hair-as-shape**: Hair is a primary silhouette element, not decoration. Six categories — cropped/close, high volume upward, wide horizontal, asymmetric fall, long contained, long uncontained. No two main characters in the same narrative arc share a hair-shape category.

**Public vs. private silhouette**:

| Dimension | Public | Private |
|---|---|---|
| Posture geometry | Vertical axis lengthened — erect, weight evenly distributed | Vertical axis compressed — weight shifts, shoulders drop |
| Perimeter | Clean boundary — clothing correct, hair composed | Softens — collar undone, hair loosened, sleeve pushed up |
| Negative space | Controlled — body occupies space intentionally | Opens or closes unpredictably — crossed arms, leaning in |
| Center of gravity | Midpoint or higher | Drops — seated, slumped, or folded inward |

**Rule: public silhouette is architectural. Private silhouette is postural.**

---

### 3.2 Facial Expression Grammar

The face is the primary performance surface. All composition rules exist to deliver the player to the face.

**Two-register vocabulary**:
- *Public expressions* are **composed** — legible from outside, performed
- *Private expressions* are **inhabited** — legible from inside, felt

**Public face rules**:
1. Resolves to a single readable social signal (happy, attentive, amused, concerned) — nameable in one word
2. Slightly too precise — expression held a beat too long, eye contact deliberate not casual
3. One **microelement** in every public expression contradicts the dominant signal — the detection mechanic's surface

**Private face rules**:
1. Does not resolve to a single signal — contains multiples (relief + grief, desire + fear)
2. Precision is gone — looser lines, shadow falls less flatly, imprecision reads as emotion
3. Eye direction shifts: public eyes *look at* (direct, intentional). Private eyes *look toward or away*.

**Minimum 9 expression states per character** (production floor, not ceiling):

| Expression | Register | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Public: Neutral/resting | Public | Default idle. Contains first microelement. |
| Public: Positive (performed warmth) | Public | Smile variant. Character-specific microelement present. |
| Public: Concerned/attentive | Public | Listening expression. Interested; holds something back. |
| Public: Guarded/deflect | Public | When a question lands too close. Surface composure, readable subtext. |
| Public: Surprised (controlled) | Public | Surprise suppressed — character catches it and recomposes. |
| Transitional: The Crack | Transitional | During The Dig. Asymmetric — one half holds public, the other is losing it. |
| Private: Unguarded positive | Private | Joy/relief/affection without social calculation. Imprecise, inhabited. |
| Private: Vulnerable | Private | Fear, grief, need, or shame. The face the character works hardest not to show. |
| Private: The Secret Face | Private | Expression specific to the character's hidden layer. Designed to be recognizable on replay. |

**The Microelement Library** (one assigned per character; no two main characters share a primary microelement):
- Periorbital tightening despite soft mouth
- Jaw set (slightly firmer than expression requires)
- Gaze orientation slightly past rather than at
- Breath-hold posture (shoulders microscopically elevated)
- Delayed expression onset (arrives one beat late)
- Smile asymmetry (one side engages fractionally less)
- Brow rest position elevated (slight perpetual lift)
- Hand position self-referential (touching own face/neck — self-soothing)

The microelement is present in every public expression. Absent in every private expression.

---

### 3.3 Environment Geometry

**Dominant geometry: soft curves with architectural interruption.**

Environments use curves that aspire to geometry but do not quite achieve it. Arches almost perfect. Furniture almost symmetrical. Gardens almost completely maintained. The physical world echoes the characters: surfaces that appear controlled but carry the evidence of the effort.

| Location Type | Dominant Shape | Interruption | Character Echo |
|---|---|---|---|
| Public social spaces | Repeated curved elements in controlled grids | One asymmetric element: a chair pushed out, unexpected shadow | The space performs too |
| Private/intimate spaces | Irregular organic geometry — no two shapes match | The interruption is the room's character | Space that belongs to someone feels like them |
| Threshold spaces (doors, stairs) | Geometric framing | Clean line; what lies beyond is less defined | Architecture of transition |
| Conflict/confrontation spaces | Angular geometry dominant — tighter angles, harder intersections | Curves reduced to edges | When a scene goes wrong, the room's softness leaves |

**Background-character geometry relationship**:
- **Public state**: Character's silhouette harmonizes with background — feels like they belong
- **The Dig**: Mild dissonance — character asymmetry against symmetric background or vice versa
- **Private/revelation**: Background recedes to near-abstraction; character is the only structured form

**Depth layers**: Far ground (soft/low detail/desaturated), mid ground (dominant geometry), near ground (sharpest detail, organic framing elements — plant, curtain, window edge — never angular).

---

### 3.4 UI Shape Grammar

**Core UI geometry: contained curves.** Rounded rectangles throughout — not pill shapes, not hard corners. Tighter radius on important/load-bearing elements; slightly more generous radius on secondary elements. One exception: the depth meter crystallization flash (see below).

**Dialogue box**:
- Horizontal rounded rectangle, lower 28–32% of screen
- Tight radius (8–12px at base resolution)
- Semi-transparent frosted material — scene visible through, character never fully occluded
- Speaker name: small pill above left edge, filled with the speaking character's public palette accent color — the only place character palette appears in UI chrome

**Buttons**:
- Primary: Filled neutral near-white → fills with character's public palette accent on hover/select. State change is color, not shape.
- Secondary: Bordered only. No fill. Hierarchy is immediate: filled = act here, bordered = available.

**Depth Meter** (the mechanic's shape):
- Not a horizontal health bar. A vertical column — 6–8px wide, ~60% of screen height, positioned at canvas edge. Nearly invisible in public states.
- Fill metaphor: **Clarifies** from opaque (bottom, unknown) to transparent (top, revealed). Color within: character's private palette anchor color.
- At tier thresholds: brief **crystallization flash** — one frame of perfect sharp-cornered rectangle — before returning to soft gradient. This is the only hard-edged geometry in the entire UI. An anomaly earns its place by being the only one.
- Bad End: color desaturates top-to-bottom (greys out, not drains).

**UI harmony rule**: No UI element uses fully sharp corners or fully soft circles. The contained-curve language is universal except the crystallization flash.

---

### 3.5 Hero Shapes vs. Supporting Shapes

**Attention hierarchy**: (1) Character's face → (2) Character's hands when expressive → (3) Relevant UI element → (4) Everything else.

**The face is the hero shape**. It must fall at or near the upper-third intersection on the canvas. Every character pose and CG must satisfy this.

| Element | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Face, expressive hands | Highest linework density; highest value contrast; most complex internal shape variation; placed at thirds intersection; never occluded by UI |
| Clothing, hair, body | Moderate linework; mid-tone dominant; slightly desaturated relative to face |
| Background | Lowest detail in the zone behind the character's head. Complexity increases only at canvas edges. |
| UI chrome | Contained-curve shapes; mid-tones; accents only through character palette fills |

**Rule**: A background must never be as visually complex as the face it frames. If a location has inherently high detail, it must be rendered out of focus, desaturated, or cropped in the character's compositional zone.

**Hands as secondary hero shape**: Drawn with face-level investment when carrying expressive content. When not expressive, simplified below face-detail standard and pushed to silhouette edge.

## 4. Color System

### 4.1 Global Palette Architecture

**World temperature: cool-neutral with earned warmth.**

The world of Masks defaults to a cool-neutral ambient (~5000–5500K). This is not a cold world — it is a composed one. The ambient communicates that the default social register is controlled and well-lit. Warmth must be earned; the world does not offer it freely.

**The world has no saturated color of its own.** Saturation comes only from characters and the semantic color vocabulary. The world is the canvas; the characters are the pigment.

**Five ambient color roles:**

| Role | Hue Family | Lightness | Saturation | Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sky Fill | Desaturated blue-grey (hue ~210–220°) | L 85–92% | S 5–12% | Dominant ambient light |
| Shadow Ambient | Desaturated violet-grey (hue ~240–250°) | L 15–28% | S 6–15% | Underlit surfaces, background recesses |
| Surface Neutral | Warm grey w/ cool tilt (hue ~30–40°, pulled neutral) | L 50–65% | S 4–8% | Default walls, floors, fabric bases |
| Threshold Warmth | Desaturated gold (hue ~35–45°) | L 60–70% | S 18–30% | Doorways, lamp pools, window edges |
| Accent Void | Near-black with cool violet pull (hue ~245–255°) | L 5–12% | S 8–20% | Confrontation spaces, Bad End frames |

---

### 4.2 Semantic Color Vocabulary

| Color | Semantic Role | Navigational? | Colorblind Backup |
|---|---|---|---|
| White / cream | Public performance, composed surface | No | No |
| Cool grey | Composure register, public base | No | No |
| Gold / warm amber | Earned trust, private beginning | Yes (depth meter) | Fill height + tier numeral + luminance flash |
| Red / crimson | Secret under pressure (expressive) | Only if in UI | Iconography + position if UI |
| Violet / purple | Withholding, unresolved interior | Only if in UI | Iconography + position if UI |
| Blue (mid-saturation) | Social distance, trust performance | No | No |
| Near-black | Foreclosed, absent (structural) | No | No |
| Rust / terracotta / blush | Embodied, unguarded, private | No | No |

**Color meanings follow the game's core logic**: surfaces that appear controlled carry social meanings. Colors that appear saturated or warm signal an interior leaking through.

**White** = performance, overexposure. **Cool grey** = the composure register; characters wear grey-blue when the mask is on. **Gold** = earned warmth — the color of a lamp at 11pm, found at proximity. **Red** = internal pressure, the secret becoming difficult to hold. **Violet** = unresolved interior; sits in the blue-red border and does not resolve. **Blue** = cultural performance of trustworthiness. **Near-black** = absence, not evil — the direction Bad End moves is away from warmth. **Rust/terracotta/blush** = the body before the performance; resists abstraction.

---

### 4.3 The Dual-Palette System

Every character has exactly two palettes. The rules below govern all characters.

#### Public Palette Constraints

1. **Temperature ceiling**: Anchor hue must fall in the cool-to-neutral range (hue 180–280° HSL, or desaturated to S ≤35%). No public anchor is warmer than desaturated terracotta.
2. **Saturation ceiling**: Anchor and field colors stay below S 35%. Accent may reach S 45% — it is the most vivid element of the public self.
3. **Lightness range**: Operates in L 45–85%.
4. **Three colors exactly**: Anchor (dominant hue, large areas) + Field (lighter/more neutral companion) + Accent (most saturated, small details only — pin, watch, name pill fill).
5. **No overlap rule**: No two characters share a public anchor within 30° of each other on the HSL wheel. Assign all hue positions before beginning character design.

#### Private Palette Constraints

1. **Temperature floor**: Private anchor must be warmer than the public anchor (+30° toward warm on HSL wheel) OR +25% saturation increase. No exceptions: characters whose interiority is grief may shift toward high-saturation near-neutral instead of warm, but they must shift.
2. **Saturation floor**: Private anchor minimum S 45%. The private self is more vivid. No exceptions.
3. **Three colors exactly**: Anchor (dominant private hue) + Shadow (deeper/warmer companion, used in private-state lighting shadows) + Highlight (light, high-saturation, used in rim lighting and depth meter fill top).
4. **The Accent Seed rule**: The private anchor is derived from the public palette's accent. Take the public accent hue, shift 15–40° toward warm, raise saturation by 25–35 points. The private self was always hiding in the smallest, most controlled detail of the public palette. On replay, players can see it.
5. **Shadow extends, highlight reveals**: Shadow = darker + warmer than public anchor. Highlight = brighter + more saturated than anything in the public palette. Should feel slightly over-lit, slightly too alive.

#### Three Palette Validation Tests (must pass before committing any pair)

1. **Warm Direction Test**: Place public anchor and private anchor side by side. The private must be warmer, more saturated, or both.
2. **Accent Seed Test**: The private anchor's hue must trace back to the public accent. Attentive players on replay should see the connection.
3. **Composition Test**: Show both palettes simultaneously (as in The Dig). They must create tension that reads as ambiguity — not cacophony.

#### Palette Construction Procedure (apply in order)

1. Assign public anchor hue (cool 180–280°, 30° clear of all other public anchors)
2. Set public anchor: S 20–35%, L 55–70%
3. Derive public field: desaturate −10–15 pts, raise lightness +15–20 pts
4. Choose public accent: any hue that reads as a controlled choice for this character (S up to 45%)
5. Derive private anchor from accent: shift hue +15–40° toward warm, raise S by +25–35 pts. Confirm S ≥ 45%.
6. Derive private shadow: shift −10–20° darker on HSL wheel, lower L −20–30 pts
7. Derive private highlight: desaturate accent −5–10 pts only, raise L +25–30 pts
8. Run all three tests. If any fails, revise from step 4.

**Colorblind validation**: Before finalizing, apply deuteranopia/protanopia simulation (Coblis, Color Oracle, Figma Accessibility). Private anchor must remain visually distinguishable from public anchor under simulation via lightness differential — revise if they converge (ensure ≥20 lightness points difference).

**Accessibility setting**: Spec a "Palette Contrast Mode" — private anchors get +15 lightness differential, depth meter gains pattern-fill texture at each tier. No structural changes, only saturation/lightness relationships.

---

### 4.4 Per-Location Color Temperature Rules

**Primary rule: Location temperature is set by game state, not geography.**

The same apartment reads cool-neutral in First Meeting and very warm in Private Moment. Relationship state owns the temperature; location surface character influences how warmth lands on it.

| Location Archetype | Surface Character | Effect When State Warms |
|---|---|---|
| Public social spaces | Cool-tinted light-grey surfaces | Warmth reads as intrusion — correct for The Dig |
| Private/domestic spaces | Neutral warm-grey surfaces | Warmth reads as natural — correct for Private Moment |
| Threshold spaces | Equal mix cool/warm surfaces | Mixed-temperature states; character is the only warm element |
| Outdoor/transitional | Cool ambient + warm point sources | Point sources = private palette; ambient = public register. Both coexist. |

**Bad End freeze rule**: When a Bad End is reached, the location's surface desaturates entirely — it loses the capacity to take warmth. The foreclosure is environmental.

---

### 4.5 UI Palette

**Core rule: UI speaks the world's language, not the characters' language — except at the exact moment of contact.**

| UI Element | Color Rule |
|---|---|
| Dialogue box fill | Semi-transparent near-white (global ambient). Never a character color. |
| Speaker name pill | Filled with speaking character's public palette accent. Only character color in UI chrome. |
| Primary button (idle) | Near-white fill, cool-grey border. |
| Primary button (hover/active) | Fills with featured character's public palette accent. |
| Secondary button | Transparent fill, cool-grey border. Active: border shifts to public accent. |
| Depth meter (inactive) | Near-transparent cool grey. Barely visible. |
| Depth meter (active) | Private palette anchor fading to private highlight at top. |
| Depth meter (crystallization flash) | One-frame white flash, sharp corners. No character color — pure luminance event. |
| Depth meter (Bad End) | All color drains to the same cool-grey as inactive state. |
| Menu backgrounds | World ambient only. No character color until a character is spotlit. |
| System UI (save/settings) | Cool grey only. Outside the diegetic fiction. |

All UI text must maintain ≥4.5:1 contrast ratio against its background at all game states. Character accent colors used in UI must be validated against text at design time; if failing, shift toward lower lightness (same hue family) until passing.

---

### 4.6 Colorblind Safety

**Rule: No meaning is carried by color alone.**

| Color Signal | Risk | Backup |
|---|---|---|
| Depth meter fill (gold/private anchor) | HIGH | Fill height + tier numeral at threshold + crystallization flash (luminance, not color) |
| Speaker name pill (character identity) | MEDIUM | Character name in text within the pill |
| Button hover state fill | LOW | 2–3% scale increase on hover + name/icon reinforcement |
| Public/private palette shift (expressive) | LOW-MEDIUM | Backed by posture geometry, linework precision changes, expression register — color deepens but does not carry the signal alone |
| Bad End atmosphere | LOW | Narrative + dialogue + depth meter at 0 + closed expression |
| Red private palette | LOW (expressive only) | No navigational role. If ever added to UI, requires icon/symbol + position or animation backup. |

## 5. Character Design Direction

### 5.1 Player Character Design

**Approach: Partial Silhouette with Selective Reveal**

The PC is never shown in full-face direct view. They appear in scenes (from behind or oblique angle ≤45°), in CGs (side profile or just past camera axis — almost looking at us, never quite), and as a visible hand presence in intimate moments.

**What is shown**: Full body in medium/long shots from rear/oblique. Hair, clothing silhouette, and hands at full detail (the player's customization surface). Face visible only in side profile in CGs.

**What is never shown**: Front-facing eyes. Any expression-based microelement. The PC has no public mask — or rather, the player is the mask.

**Customization surface**:
- Proportion tier: Narrow / Standard / Wide (same three tiers as the cast)
- Skin tone: Six values across warm-to-cool range, all within world palette
- Hair: Five silhouette shapes (cropped/close, high volume upward, wide horizontal, asymmetric fall, long contained). Long uncontained is reserved for love interests.
- Eye color: Visible in profile CGs only — three options (cool/neutral/warm)

**Palette treatment — the PC as visual neutral**: The PC has no dual palette. Single wardrobe pool (8–12 options), all within world cool-neutral ambient, none exceeding S 30%. The PC must never compete visually with a love interest's palette reveal — they are the surface warmth arrives at, not the source.

**CG appearance rules**:
- PC hands appear in 30–40% of CGs; always at face-level render quality
- In close CG moments, ear/jaw/back of neck visible — rendered at face-close quality standard
- PC silhouette never dominates; love interest face at upper-thirds intersection, PC as framing element

---

### 5.2 Love Interest Design Archetypes

Five archetypes based on **what the mask looks like** — the type of performance, not the personality. Every character's archetype is assigned before the first sketch. No two characters in the same narrative arc share an archetype.

---

**Archetype 1: The Polished Surface**
Flawless, expensive, deliberate. The mask is indistinguishable from a very good person with excellent taste.
- Silhouette: Vertical dominance. Clean fall, minimal layering, centered weight.
- Proportion: Narrow or Standard.
- Expression: Composed warmth — pleasant, slightly too still. Smile arrives and leaves at the correct moment.
- Public palette: Cool blue-grey or blue-green (hue 190–240°). Accent is a precise single piece (watch, cufflink, contained gem).
- Setup for reveal: Precision is the tell. Any imprecision — a hair out of place, a pause before the practiced smile — reads immediately as signal.
- Avoid: Making "polished" read as cold. This mask is warm. It has been rehearsed.

---

**Archetype 2: The Occupied Person**
Too busy to perform — and performing that bustle. The mask is presented as the absence of a mask.
- Silhouette: Asymmetry. Bag strap, sleeve pushed, hair functional or in motion.
- Proportion: Standard or Wide.
- Expression: Warm distraction — open face, frequent gestures, brief genuine eye contact before moving on. The warmth is real; the motion is the mask.
- Public palette: Warm neutrals — desaturated ochre, olive, muted terracotta (hue 30–60°). Saturation strictly ≤35%.
- Setup for reveal: When the character is entirely still, it reads as unusual. The private reveal often involves them stopping.
- Avoid: Motion reading as anxiety. Energy must be appetitive, not avoidant.

---

**Archetype 3: The Understatement**
Minimal, quiet, easy to overlook. The mask is performed absence.
- Silhouette: Compact with high-detail center. Monochromatic clothing recedes; all complexity at the face.
- Proportion: Narrow.
- Expression: Attentive neutrality — patient, listening quality. Not blank; specifically attending.
- Public palette: Most desaturated in the cast. Cool grey range (hue 210–250°, S 10–22%). Accent may be nearly invisible at thumbnail.
- Setup for reveal: Private palette arrives from maximum desaturation — contrast ratio is the highest of any archetype. Lean into private anchor S 70–80%.
- Avoid: Blank or passive. This character is watching closely. They are simply not showing it.

---

**Archetype 4: The Social Chameleon**
Warm, immediately appealing, highly readable. Performed emotional availability.
- Silhouette: Horizontal weight. Open gestures, expressive shoulders, stance that faces toward.
- Proportion: Wide or Standard.
- Expression: High-bandwidth warmth — mobile, fast, large. Expressions arrive faster than other archetypes. In a cast where everyone else is slightly too still, this character is slightly too expressive.
- Public palette: Warm-adjacent cool (hue 160–200°, blue-green). Reads as warm because it is the warmest cool available.
- Setup for reveal: Private reveal shows the character stopping the performance. Private expressions are the smallest in the cast. The person who showed you everything was showing you nothing.
- Avoid: Warmth reading as fake or sycophantic. The mask must be good enough to work.

---

**Archetype 5: The Categorically Unclear**
Does not resolve. Age unclear. Gender presentation actively ambiguous. Social role inconsistent. The mask is irresolvability itself.
- Silhouette: Irregular perimeter. Hair volume, layered clothing, soft boundary.
- Proportion: Any — but the tier must be visually obscured by silhouette irregularity.
- Expression: Present absence — face slightly turned, eyes directed at something not in frame, expression a beat behind.
- Public palette: Any hue range, but unusually wide internal temperature spread. Accent at S 48–50% (most saturated in cast) — the private color in plain sight.
- Setup for reveal: Revelation is not warmth arriving — it is resolution arriving. The private palette collapses the temperature spread; the character becomes legible for the first time.
- Avoid: Reading as eerie or uncanny. This is pleasant irresolvability — someone interesting, not unsettling.

---

### 5.3 Distinguishing Feature Rules

Every character has one structurally unusual facial feature — anatomical, permanent, visible at 80×160px thumbnail.

**What counts in this style** (deviates measurably from style default geometry):

| Category | Counts | Does Not Count |
|---|---|---|
| Eye geometry | Pronounced epicanthal shape, deep set, single lid one eye only, measurable size asymmetry | Color variation, iris pattern |
| Jawline | Notably sharp, notably round beyond default, prominent offset | Shading variation |
| Nose | Strongly upturned, broad bridge, long bridge, near-absent dorsal | Shading only |
| Brow | Strong arch, nearly horizontal, sparse/absent visual presence | Color variation |
| Mouth | Width >65% of face width, extreme cupid's bow | Lipstick, shading |
| Proportions | Forehead dominance, chin recess, unusually long philtrum | Overall stylization level |
| Ears | Visible, prominent, unusual size/shape in default facing | Only if visible at thumbnail |

**One feature per character.** Two unusual features produces caricature.

**Off-limits**:
- Inhuman proportions (pupil-to-face ratio >35%, head width >110% of shoulders in any tier)
- Visible surgical alteration as aesthetic design choice (narrative element requiring director sign-off if present)
- Features functioning as ethnic type markers — any feature derived from specific ethnic physiognomy must be drawn from a pool distributed across the full cast, never assigned to a single character as a type marker
- Any feature combination producing a face outside the "inviting" half of the visual rule

---

### 5.4 Clothing as Character Information

**Public clothing is a thesis statement. Private clothing is its retraction.**

**Public clothing rules**:
1. Every outfit communicates one specific social claim (state this claim in one sentence before designing). The claim determines formality, fit geometry, and accessory logic.
2. One statement piece per outfit — all other elements neutral. Statement piece carries the public accent color.
3. Clothing fit echoes silhouette signature. Deliberate contradiction is permitted but must be documented.
4. If the claim cannot be stated in one sentence — revise the outfit.

**Private clothing = functional undress**: The same outfit, partially disassembled. One to three elements of the public outfit removed or altered — jacket off, tie loose, top button open, hair released. The alteration must address the statement piece directly. What remains visible carries the private palette — the underlying layers are in private anchor or shadow color.

**Private clothing must NOT**:
- Be a completely different outfit (continuity of the same clothing disassembled is the visual argument)
- Read as costume or replacement fantasy wardrobe
- Introduce private-palette elements with no visible source in the public outfit

**Universal anchor object**: Every character has exactly one accessory that appears unchanged in both public and private states. Present whether character is fully composed or fully undone. Rules:
- Small enough not to dominate sprite composition (ring, pendant, watch, wristband, hair clip)
- In public: carries the public accent color
- In private: rendered at slightly higher detail and contrast than in public — the attentive player notices it
- Narrative significance set by narrative director; design team ensures visual consistency and private-state legibility
- No two characters' anchor objects are the same item category

---

### 5.5 LOD and Consistency Rules

**Rendering standards**:

| Scene Type | Standard |
|---|---|
| Standard sprite | Face: full line density. Body: moderate linework, simplified folds. Hair: volume clear, individual strands at face boundary only. |
| Close sprite (depth tier transitions) | +15% line density on face and visible hands |
| Standard CG | Full render throughout. Hair at full individual detail. |
| Intimate CG (Private Moment) | Full render + 8–12% global line weight reduction pass + slight edge softening on face contour. Underlying drawing must be at full quality before loosening. |

**Thumbnail test (mandatory before sprite approval)**: Resize to 80×160px. Must be immediately readable without prior knowledge: (1) silhouette signature, (2) distinguishing feature presence, (3) general expression register (positive/neutral/guarded/private).

**Construction document** — created before any expressions are drawn:
1. Face geometry diagram with measurement anchors (eye center height, nose tip, mouth center, cheekbone width, jaw width) — does not change between expressions
2. Color reference strip (public palette, private palette, skin tone, lip tones)
3. Hair geometry template — volume shape drawn flat; all expressions derive from this
4. Microelement reference panel at 3× scale — every public expression checked against it (present), every private expression checked (absent)
5. Silhouette signature at 80×160px — all sprites checked against it

**Cross-expression change rules**:
- Must change: expression geometry, eye direction, linework precision, mouth geometry
- Must stay consistent: face measurements, distinguishing feature, hair mass shape (except approved loosened variant), skin tone, accessory presence
- May vary: clothing state, individual hair stroke work within mass template, posture

**Returning artist protocol**: If a new expression is drawn by a different artist, or >4 weeks after the original set — derive from the construction document, not from prior expression drawings. Prior drawings carry accumulated drift.

## 6. Environment Design Language

### 6.1 Setting Context

**Contemporary undated city.** No visible technology more specific than a flat screen. No branded storefronts. No seasonal holiday markers. A composite of real contemporary urban life — coffee shops, apartments, parks, workplaces, rooftops, university buildings — with all specific time/place markers removed. The player cannot place the story in a specific year or city.

**Visual register: aspirational-ordinary.** Locations are slightly better-lit and more considered than real life — the world as it would look if everyone were performing a curated life at 85% capacity. Not fantasy-luxurious. Not dystopian. The light that flatters a person in a well-chosen photograph.

**Architecture**: Contemporary urban, Northern/Western European or East Asian visual vocabulary — clean geometries, minimal ornament, an appreciation for light quality. No dominant cultural marker. Natural light preference (large windows, glass walls, open plans). Scale convention: human scale throughout, approximately "standing in the room" — the scale of a conversation, not spectacle.

---

### 6.2 Background Production Approach

**Method: Painted backgrounds, flat-base digital workflow.** Not photo-composite (breaks texture match with linework characters). Not 3D-rendered-then-painted (incompatible with MVP budget and artist skill set).

**Layer structure per background** (artist deliverable spec):

| Layer | Content | State-modulated? |
|---|---|---|
| Base Structure | Flat-color architecture/geometry, no lighting | No — static |
| Lighting Pass | Directional light, ambient wash | YES — primary state-shift layer |
| Detail Objects | Environmental storytelling props | No — static |
| Atmospheric Overlay | Dust, soft glow, haze | YES — secondary state-shift layer |
| Foreground Frame | Near-layer organic element (curtain, plant, railing edge) | No — static |

State variants are exported as Lighting Pass + Atmospheric Overlay swaps, not entirely repainted backgrounds. Artists deliver one structural painting per location; state variants are lighting-layer composites.

**Mandatory state variants per location**:

| Variant | When Used | What Changes |
|---|---|---|
| Neutral Day | First Meeting, Surface Relationship | Even cool-neutral lighting pass |
| Charged Transition | The Dig | Mixed temperature, competing sources; slight haze overlay |
| Private Night | Revelation, Private Moment | Single warm source dominant, near-dark ambient; soft bloom |
| Foreclosed | Bad End | Flat cold overcast; atmosphere removed; base desaturation filter |

A 5th "Public Theater" variant (Main Menu/creator spaces) is required only for the Main Menu location — not the full inventory.

**CG scenes** are fully painted single-image compositions with baked lighting. They are not modular and exist outside the state system.

---

### 6.3 Location Vocabulary — Component Library Approach

**The "20 bedrooms" problem is solved by the Component Library.**

**Two-tier system**:
- **Tier 1: Shared Social Spaces** — fully designed, owned by no single character, used across multiple routes. High production investment.
- **Tier 2: Personal Spaces** — architectural shell from the shared vocabulary + Character Detail Overlay unique to that character.

**Architectural Shell Library**:

| Shell | Variants | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Urban Bedroom | 2 (compact/spacious) | Neutral wall, bed, window right, desk left — no detail objects |
| Urban Living/Common Area | 2 (modern/lived-in) | Sofa zone, window, open door to kitchen |
| Study / Work Space | 2 (institutional/personal) | Desk, shelving, window, overhead light |
| Corridor / Transitional | 1 | Hallway, threshold framing, ambiguous destination |
| Exterior: Street Level | 2 (day/night lighting base) | Urban street, no specific identifying features |
| Exterior: Elevated | 1 | Rooftop / fire escape / balcony vocabulary |

Six shell types, ten variants. Every personal space = one shell + one Character Detail Overlay.

**Character Detail Overlay** (what makes a space belong to someone):
- 3–6 character-specific props (see Section 6.4 storytelling techniques)
- Color temperature preset biased toward the character's private palette
- One structural modification (books stacked instead of shelved, plants overrunning a corner, one incongruous object)
- Single layer, composited on top of shell. If character arc ends, overlay archives; shell reuses.

**Shared public space inventory (MVP + expansion)**:
Café interior / University corridor / Bar-lounge interior / Park path exterior / Building lobby / Classroom or seminar room

---

### 6.4 Environmental Storytelling Techniques

**The room knows what its occupant is hiding. The player earns the right to read it.**

Five techniques designed to reward replayers without penalizing first-time players:

**Technique 1 — The Displaced Object**: One object in every personal space is in the wrong place. Slightly wrong — not dramatically. A kitchen item in the bedroom. A photo facing the wall. A chair toward a corner. On first play: idiosyncrasy. On replay: the private behavior it records is explained. *Production note: document the object and its explanation in the character sheet. Artists must know what it means.*

**Technique 2 — The Excessive Organizing System**: Characters with control-based secrets have one zone over-organized beyond ordinary reason. Its counterpart is always present: one surrendered zone where the effort to maintain order was abandoned. The contrast between them is the character.

**Technique 3 — The Visible Past**: Every personal space has one element visually older than the rest of the room — a temporally incongruous piece of furniture, a worn object in a curated space, a photograph with a different color temperature. On first play: atmospheric texture. On replay: the backstory source.

**Technique 4 — The Anticipatory Arrangement**: In scenes preceding a Revelation, the background's dominant light source points toward where the character will stand during the reveal. Background artists must receive a compositional brief telling them where the confrontation happens. The background prepares the stage.

**Technique 5 — The Contradiction Object**: One object in every personal space is directly contradicted by the character's performed identity. A self-sufficient character with a shelf of community/care books. An unsentimental character with a carefully preserved useless collection. *Production note: requires art director sign-off per character. Must be specific enough to read as contradiction after the secret is known; generic enough not to announce itself before.*

---

### 6.5 Background/Character Integration Rules

**Edge handling — the Depth Haze Rule**: Character sprites never sit hard-edged against fully sharp backgrounds. The near-ground layer provides a soft organic framing element (plant edge, curtain fall, table corner) partially overlapping the character's lower body.

Background linework in the head/shoulder compositional zone must have line weight ≤50% of the character's face linework weight. If architectural elements in that zone require heavy lines, introduce a mid-ground element (translucent fabric, interior haze, atmospheric overlay) to break the weight.

**Depth treatment** (no photographic gaussian blur):
- Line weight reduces at distance: far ground has minimal or no linework (color only)
- Saturation reduces at distance: far ground −20 to −35% relative to mid-ground
- Detail complexity reduces at distance: far ground is simple geometric form only
- Near-ground: full line weight, maximum detail, but desaturated relative to character

**Rule**: The character sprite must always be the sharpest, most line-rich, most saturated element in the frame.

**Lighting consistency**: Background light direction must match sprite light direction within ±30°. Background artists and character sprite artists share a lighting brief per location per state variant.

**State transitions**: Backgrounds transition through Lighting Pass swap, not cross-dissolve. The geometry stays constant; the light changes. Cross-dissolves used only between different locations.

**Color temperature relationship**: Public states — background and sprite temperature agree within ±200K. The Dig — deliberate split permitted (background slightly cooler; character "warmer than the room"). Private/Revelation — background may drop to near-dark while character is lit by a warmer point source.

## 7. UI/HUD Visual Direction

### 7.1 Diegetic vs. Screen-Space

**All UI is screen-space. No diegetic elements.**

Diegetic UI would give characters a performed surface the player reads through — the exact register to avoid for mechanical information. The depth meter especially must never feel like something characters control or perform; it is the player's private instrument, invisible to the cast.

The depth meter's near-invisibility in public states *implies* diegesis without claiming it: it reads as something the player knows that the characters do not.

**In-scene UI vocabulary**: Dialogue box, speaker name pill, depth meter, choice overlay. Nothing else appears during a scene.

---

### 7.2 Typography Direction

**System: Humanist sans-serif for body text + transitional serif for display/accent.**

The dual-typeface system mirrors the game's duality. The sans-serif handles all reading-volume text (legible, unobtrusive, warm without decoration). The serif appears only in speaker names, chapter titles, and logotype — it carries the "public face is a performance" quality.

**Typeface directions** (final selection requires license review):
- Body sans-serif: Humanist sans in the tradition of Gill Sans, Optima, or Lato — organic stroke contrast, reads as *written*, not engineered
- Display serif: Transitional serif with slightly tapered serifs and moderate x-height, e.g. Libre Baskerville or Cormorant Garamond Regular — a reading serif used decoratively

**Weight Hierarchy:**

| Text Role | Typeface | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| Dialogue body | Sans-serif | Regular (400) — never bold |
| Speaker name | Serif | Regular or Medium (400–500) |
| Choice options (idle) | Sans-serif | Medium (500) |
| Choice options (hover/focus) | Sans-serif | SemiBold (600) |
| UI labels (save/load/settings) | Sans-serif | Light (300) |
| Chapter/act titles | Serif | Regular (400), generous leading |
| Main menu logotype | Serif | Regular, tracked +60–80 |

**Size Hierarchy at 1920×1080:**

| Text Role | Size |
|---|---|
| Dialogue body | 22–24px (**floor: 16px minimum — do not go below for sustained reading**) |
| Speaker name | 15–16px |
| Choice options | 20–22px |
| UI labels (system) | 16–18px |
| Chapter titles | 36–40px |
| Main menu logotype | 80–100px |

**Dialogue text column**: Cap at 55–65 characters per line (approximately 65% of box width). The visible breathing room on each side reinforces the frosted box as a frame, not a container.

**Leading**: 1.5× line height for dialogue (33–36px at 22px text). **This is a hard floor — do not tighten.** 30–60 minute reading sessions require generous leading.

**Tracking**: Default (0) for body text. Speaker name: +20–30. Chapter titles: +40–60. Logotype: +60–80. Never tighten body text below default.

---

### 7.3 Iconography Style

**Style: Outlined, single-weight, organically rounded.**

Clean outlined icons at a single stroke weight. Corners softened to match the contained-curve UI grammar. Subordinate to text — icons serve as navigation landmarks, not primary communication.

- **Stroke weight**: 1.5–2px at base resolution. Consistent across all icons.
- **Rounded terminals**: r=2px minimum on all endpoints and corners. No hard terminals.
- **No fills**: Outline only. Active/selected icon receives a contained-curve background fill in near-white at 15% opacity (same frosted material as the dialogue box).
- **Icon set**: Save, Load, Settings, Back/Return, Skip, Auto, Menu. Seven icons. No decorative icons.
- **Size**: 24×24px at base. Touch/gamepad hit target: 44×44px minimum (with padding).

---

### 7.4 UI Animation Feel

**Language: Deliberate and unhurried, with one anomaly.**

All transitions are ~15–20% slower than genre convention (~180–200ms vs. standard VN ~150ms). This creates the impression UI is being presented, not executing.

| Element | Enter | Exit | Duration | Easing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dialogue box | Fade + upward translate +8px | Fade out | 180ms / 120ms | Ease-out / ease-in |
| Speaker name pill | Fade, +40ms delay after box | Fades with box | 140ms | Ease-out |
| Dialogue text | Character-by-character, 30–40ms/character (settings-adjustable) | Snaps out with box | — | Linear |
| Choice overlay | Box fades, options stagger top-to-bottom +60ms each | Fade as unit | 160ms box / 60ms stagger | Ease-out |
| Depth meter tier flash | **Instant** (one frame) → slow fade of crystallized state | — | 600ms post-flash | Ease-in fade |
| Menu transitions | Cross-fade | — | 220ms | Linear |
| Button hover fill | Color wipes in from left edge | Wipes back on unhover | 120ms | Ease-in-out |

**The crystallization flash is the only instant event.** Everything else eases. Do not add other snap animations. The flash earns its meaning by being the only one.

**Depth meter is ambient** (player checks it; flash rewards noticing, does not demand attention). Edge position is correct.

**Keyboard/gamepad**: Focus traversal triggers the button hover fill wipe animation without mouse movement. Gamepad d-pad activates the same wipe on focus.

**Reduced motion accessibility**: All translate-based animations resolve to fade-only. Durations unchanged. Text reveal: optional "full line fade-in" at equivalent total duration.

---

### 7.5 Choice / Dialogue Option Visual Design

**Shape**: Horizontal rounded-rect button (r=8–12px), matching text column width. No icons, no visible numbering.

**Layout**: Vertical stack replacing the dialogue text area. Speaker name pill and box frame remain. Maximum 4 choices visible — no scrolling. If narrative requires >4 choices, escalate to narrative director for restructuring.

**States:**

| State | Fill | Border | Text |
|---|---|---|---|
| Idle | Near-white, 85% opacity | Cool-grey, 60%, 1px | Sans-serif Medium (500), cool near-black |
| Hover (mouse) | Character public accent, 80% (wipe from left) | Accent, 100% | SemiBold (600), white or near-black (pre-calculated per accent for ≥4.5:1 contrast) |
| Focused (keyboard/gamepad) | Character public accent, 80% (wipe from left) + **left-edge accent bar (3px, accent color, full option height)** | Accent, 100% | SemiBold (600) |
| Active/Pressed | Accent, 100% opaque | Accent, 100% | Maximum contrast value, hold 80ms |

**Keyboard navigation**: Left-edge accent bar (3px, accent color, full option height) is the focus indicator for all interactive elements — consistent with choice option pattern, applied universally.

**Consequence flagging**: No visual system for flagging choice weight/consequence by default. If the narrative director requests it, the visual solution is a Light-weight secondary text line at 14px below the choice text — never a color or icon change.

---

### 7.6 Menu Systems

**Menus are threshold spaces. They use the world's ambient language.**

**Scene-to-menu transition**: The current background desaturates toward world ambient cool-neutral over 300ms before the menu overlay appears. "Threshold warmth withdraws, reversibly."

---

**Main Menu**:
- Background: Main Menu Space (Section 6.6 Background #09, Public Theater variant). No characters until New Game flow.
- Logotype "Masks": centered-upper at ~30–35% from top. Serif Regular, tracked +60–80.
- Menu items: text-only vertical list. Sans-serif Regular (400), 22–24px, cool-grey near-black. No button fill shapes.
- Hover: single underline in threshold gold (Threshold Warmth color from Section 4.1). Only place gold appears in meta UI.
- No animated character silhouettes or parallax. The stillness of the main menu is intentional — characters are behind the door.

---

**Settings**:
- Full-screen overlay over a 70% cool-grey scrim.
- Panel: centered rounded-rect (r=12px), ~60% screen width, ~70% screen height. Near-white fill, 95% opacity. No frosted material — settings is outside the diegetic fiction.
- Header: "Settings" in serif Regular, 28px, left-aligned, 24px internal padding. Back icon top-right.
- Category navigation: left-rail. Light (300), 16px. Selected: Medium (500) + left accent bar in **cool-grey** (not character accent — system UI is cool grey only).
- **No character color anywhere in settings.** System UI is cool grey only.
- Interactive state visibility: left-edge accent bar in cool-grey serves as the focus indicator throughout settings. Near-white focus ring against cool-grey background for high-contrast focus signal without character color.

---

**Save / Load**:
- Same overlay structure (dim scrim + centered panel). Panel wider: ~75% screen width.
- Layout: 3-column grid of save slot cards.
- Slot card content: 160×90px full-color scene thumbnail (state color is part of the save record), location name (Regular 14px), date-time (Light 12px), last dialogue line preview (Regular 13px italic, truncated with ellipsis).
- Empty slot: dashed border, "Empty" in Light (300) 14px, cool-grey 50%.
- Hover/focus state: border thickens 1px → 2px, background +5% opacity. No character color.
- Auto-save badge: "Auto" text badge (Light 12px, cool-grey), lower-left of thumbnail. Text only, no icon.
- Overwrite confirmation modal: appears within the panel as a card atop a semi-transparent scrim over the slot grid. "Save" (primary button, cool-grey palette only) + "Cancel" (secondary button).

---

### 7.7 Dialogue Box Transparency — Safety Specification

**Minimum opaque fill floor**: The frosted dialogue box must include a minimum opaque fill component of **≥65%** regardless of the frosted/blur layer above it. This ensures text contrast is maintained across all 7 mood states, particularly State 3 (The Dig — competing light sources), State 4 (Revelation — warm directional source near dialogue area), and State 7 (Bad End — desaturated flat scene).

The ≥4.5:1 text contrast requirement from Section 4.5 is enforced by this floor, not by relying solely on the frosted blur effect against dynamic backgrounds.

**Implementation note**: Structure the dialogue box as: (1) a solid-fill rect at ≥65% opacity in the box's near-white color, then (2) a frosted/blur effect layer above it. The blur adds visual softness; the solid fill guarantees contrast. Do not rely on blur alone.

### 8.4 Texture Compression (PC target)

| Asset Type | Format | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Character sprites (with transparency) | **BC7** | Visually lossless at 8 bits/px. Preserves soft linework edges and expression microdetail. DXT5/BC3 fallback only for mobile future-proofing. |
| CG illustrations (no transparency) | BC1 (DXT1) | Halves memory cost; acceptable on painted art with no alpha. Use BC7 if any CG has semi-transparent elements. |
| Background base layer (opaque) | BC1 | |
| Background lighting/atmosphere variants (with alpha) | BC7 | Soft transparency edges require BC7 — BC1 will ruin them. |
| UI elements (fine text edges, transparency) | BC7 | Or uncompressed (RGBA32) for small frequently-used elements under 256×256. |

> **Verify BC7 availability**: Confirm BC7 is available for the *Sprite* texture type in Unity 6.3 import settings — may differ from the *Default* texture type. Reference: `docs/engine-reference/unity/VERSION.md` sources.

**Pipeline rule**: Source PNGs exported from painting software must use **straight (unassociated) alpha**. Enable "Alpha Is Transparency" in Unity's texture import settings — Unity handles premultiplication at import. Exporting premultiplied PNGs will cause double-premultiplication and washed-out transparent edges. This is a mandatory art onboarding rule.

---

### 8.5 Color Space

**Working**: sRGB throughout the art production pipeline.

All dual-palette construction, palette validation tests, and colorblind simulation must be performed in sRGB viewing conditions. The HSL-defined color system in Section 4 is perceptual — artists working in sRGB see and mix the palette values the art bible defines.

**Engine**: Unity 6.3 URP defaults to **Linear** color space. All color-data textures (sprites, CGs, backgrounds, UI) import as sRGB — Unity converts to linear at sample time. Do not change the project color space to Gamma.

**Common issue**: Mid-tone colors appear slightly darker in Unity's Game View than in Photoshop's standard preview. Fix: artists use a color-managed environment (Photoshop with sRGB document profile) and validate final colors against Unity's Game View, not painting software alone.

---

### 8.6 Sprite Sheets vs. Individual Files

**Individual files for all character expressions. Unity Sprite Atlas handles atlasing at build time.**

Rationale:
- The Section 5.5 construction document and microelement consistency checks work expression-by-expression — individual files support this; sprite sheets obscure it
- When a single expression fails review, individual files allow surgical replacement. A sheet means re-exporting and re-importing the whole atlas.
- Unity's Sprite Atlas system auto-atlases individual files at build time, preserving draw-call efficiency without baking production workflow decisions into artist deliverables.

**Sprite Atlas strategy (engine)**: One atlas per character, containing all expressions. Load/unload via Addressables per scene. At any given scene, ≤5 characters = ≤5 active atlases = draw call cost of ≤5 for all character draws combined (assuming SRP Batcher compatibility — see Section 8.7).

---

### 8.7 Draw Call Budget and Batching Rules

**Budget at ≤100 draw calls/frame:**

| System | Target | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Background layers (3 composited) | 3 | One per layer — not cross-batchable |
| Active characters (up to 5) | 1–5 | SRP-Batcher-compatible if same material |
| UI (dialogue, HUD) | 1–3 | UI Toolkit batches aggressively per UIDocument |
| Post-processing passes | 2–4 | URP volume passes |
| Misc overhead | 5–10 | |
| **Total target** | **12–25** | Well within budget |

**Rules that preserve batching:**
1. All character SpriteRenderers must use the **same URP material** (Sprites-Default or one custom shared Sprite-Lit material). Per-instance variation must use `MaterialPropertyBlock`, not material duplication.
2. Do not use `SpriteRenderer.color` for tinting. Use `MaterialPropertyBlock` on the shared material.
3. Background layers must not use per-location custom materials unless the effect is global.
4. **Use UI Toolkit (UIDocument) for dialogue and HUD — not UGUI Canvas.** UI Toolkit batches all geometry from one UIDocument into minimal draw calls. UGUI canvas with dynamic elements (dialogue text changing every frame) forces canvas rebuilds and inflates draw call cost.

---

### 8.8 Background State Variant Implementation

**Separate sprites composited at runtime. Do not pre-composite.**

Three layers per background as separate sprites on separate sorting layers:
1. `BackgroundBase` — Opaque, always visible, BC1
2. `LightingPass` — Additive or multiply blend, BC7 with alpha. Swapped per game state.
3. `AtmosphericOverlay` — Soft transparency, BC7 with alpha. Swapped per mood.

Load only the active base + current lighting/atmosphere pair via Addressables. Release prior scene assets before loading next.

**Why not pre-composited**: 9 locations × 4 variants × 8MB (BC7) = 288MB if all loaded. The layer approach stores 9 bases (3.5MB each = 31.5MB) + only the active variants. In-scene texture load stays under 100MB.

---

### 8.9 Memory Budget

**Realistic in-scene footprint (correct approach — Addressables streaming):**

| Asset Group | Active Count | Per Asset | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Background base (current scene) | 1 | 3.5MB BC1 | 3.5MB |
| Active lighting variant | 1 | 8MB BC7 | 8MB |
| Active atmosphere variant | 1 | 8MB BC7 | 8MB |
| Character atlases (up to 5 in scene) | 5 | 8MB BC7 | 40MB |
| Current CG (if applicable) | 1 | 8MB BC7 | 8MB |
| UI atlas (always loaded) | 1 | 8MB BC7 | 8MB |
| Engine / audio / scripts overhead | — | — | ~200–400MB |
| **Realistic in-scene total** | | | **~75MB textures + overhead** |

Well within the 2GB ceiling. The danger scenario (all 30 character atlases + all variants + all CGs loaded simultaneously) approaches ~783MB for textures alone — entirely preventable with explicit Addressables load/unload at scene transitions.

**Hard rules:**
- Never pre-load the full cast. Load character atlases on-demand per scene.
- CG textures are loaded immediately before display and released immediately after.
- In-scene active texture memory target: **under 100MB at any one time**.

## 9. Reference Direction

*This section names four reference sources. Each points at a specific visual problem Masks has; none overlap. Use these as precise instruments, not mood boards. For each, explicit divergence rules are listed to prevent the game from reading as derivative.*

---

### Reference 1 — *Disco Elysium* (ZA/UM, 2019)

**Primary section informed**: Mood & Atmosphere (Section 2) / Environment Design Language (Section 6)

**What to draw from — the painted world as psychological state**: *Disco Elysium* uses painterly backgrounds not for nostalgia but as argument — the rendering style IS the narrator's unreliability. Brushwork loosens and tightens based on what the protagonist can emotionally bear to see clearly. Extract this specific technique: backgrounds should change their rendering *quality* (line precision, detail density) across the game state system, not just their color temperature. In First Meeting, the environment is rendered at its crispest — over-described, slightly airless. In Private Moment, it falls toward sketch quality in the same way linework on private expressions does. The world softens when the mask comes off.

Specific application: the atmospheric overlay layer (Section 6.2) gains permission to blur and simplify detail objects, not just shift temperature. This is not stylistic inconsistency; it is the same logic as expression register loosening.

**What to diverge from**: *Disco Elysium*'s color is expressionist and saturated — the world screams. Masks operates in the opposite direction: the world is desaturated so the characters can carry all saturation. Do not let backgrounds develop independent color personalities. The painted world of *Disco Elysium* is the protagonist; in Masks, the world is the stage and must remain subordinate.

---

### Reference 2 — *I Love Yoo* (Quimchee, 2018–present)

**Primary section informed**: Character Design Direction (Section 5) / Shape Language (Section 3)

**What to draw from — the microexpression as primary communication surface**: Quimchee's character work is built around faces that carry visible social strain — expressions where one layer of feeling is performing over another. The specific technique to extract is the **simultaneous register**: a character in *I Love Yoo* can be smiling while the periorbital geometry reads as something entirely different. Study how this is achieved structurally: the mouth operates on one plane; the eye area is drawn as though it belongs to a different emotional state entirely. This is the visual grammar Section 3.2 describes as the "one-word public readable, multivalent private" system — but Quimchee demonstrates how to draw it.

Also extract: proportional economy. Characters are elongated but not fantasy-elaborate. Gesture and silhouette carry significant weight precisely because the rendering is restrained and the lines are not overwrought. When a hand moves, it means something.

**What to diverge from**: *I Love Yoo*'s panel-to-panel storytelling logic (stagger, time skip, manga-grammar rhythm) does not transfer to VN. The reference is purely for single-image expression geometry. Do not derive compositional framing or staging from manhwa panel grids — the VN canvas is a theatrical proscenium, not a scroll panel.

---

### Reference 3 — *Portrait of a Lady on Fire* (Céline Sciamma, dir., 2019)

**Primary section informed**: Color System — Dual-Palette System and Mood States (Sections 4.2, 4.3, 2)

**What to draw from — earned warmth and the firelight as private register**: The film's color language is structurally identical to the dual-palette system: daylight scenes are cool, controlled, socially surveilled. Candlelight and firelight are the only warm-temperature sources — and they appear exclusively in scenes of private intimacy. The warmth is not decorative; it signals where observation has been withdrawn. The film enforces this rigorously: warmth is literally rationed.

The specific technique to extract is the **single-source private light**. Revelation states (Section 2, State 4) should be lit as though by one source the character carries with them — the same quality as a hand holding a candle: directional, close, warm, slightly unsteady. Not dramatic cinematic key light. Human-scale. The character does not feel *lit*; they feel *found*.

**What to diverge from**: The film's visual language is about two people in reciprocal attention — the gaze is mutual and acknowledged. Masks is asymmetric: the player reads the character; the character manages being read. Do not let the reference push CG compositions toward equality of gaze or shared framing. Love interest faces should always be the compositional subject, not one of two equal elements.

---

### Reference 4 — *In the Mood for Love* (Wong Kar-wai, dir., 2000)

**Primary section informed**: Environment Design Language — Background/Character Integration, Depth Treatment (Sections 6.5, 3.3)

**What to draw from — the frame as complicit withholding**: Wong Kar-wai shoots characters through doorways, through beaded curtains, from around corners — the environment is consistently interposed between the camera and the subject. The character is partially occluded, partially revealed, and the physical world participates in the act of concealment. The **near-ground framing element** described in Section 6.5 (plant edge, curtain fall, table corner partially overlapping the character's lower body) is not a spatial depth cue or a rendering convention. It is the world performing the same withholding the character is performing.

The foreground frame element (Section 6.2, Foreground Frame layer) is permitted to partially occlude the character in charged and private states. In First Meeting, the frame is decorative. In The Dig, it begins to interpose. In Private Moment, it may cut across the composition actively. The level of foreground intrusion is a secondary indicator of game state.

**What to diverge from**: The film's pace and repetition use time as a material — the same composition returning across scenes to accumulate meaning. VN cannot use temporal repetition the same way. Additionally, the film withholds without the intention of eventual revelation; Masks withholds with the explicit promise of earned access. The foreground occlusion must ease and recede as depth increases — it is a system of graduated withholding, not obstruction.
